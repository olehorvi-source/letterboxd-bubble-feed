2026-10-25 Exorcist II: The Heretic (1977) brofromanother primary no The standard cinephile line is that Ex 2 functions as a corrective to its predecessor - that Boorman’s earnest, flailing folly stands in opposition to Friedkin’s savvy, machine-tooled brutality. There’s something to this insofar as Boorman more or less went on the record about his against-the-grain intentions, while Friedkin registered his displeasure with the results in the same mocking terms as the majority of film critics descending, Pazazu-like, on a DOA sequel: “the worst piece of shit I’ve ever seen..a fucking disgrace… your mother sucks cocks in hell, Boorman.” Me, I’m built different. I like both movies, and also Blatty’s weird and wacky Exorcist III - itself a departure, heralding a decade and change of demonic urban- inferno procedurals (Seven, Cure, etc). My fondness for The Heretic is bound up mostly in its aesthetics, which are - as per authorities from Pauline Kael to Martin Scorsese - flatly stunning, though it’s worth noting that they often honour and extrapolate on the source material: the tug of war over Regan’s heart echoes the shot of her in the original reaching out while the statue of Pazazu lurks behind her. The sleepwalking sequence atop the skyscraper is so beautiful: modernism refracted and made uncanny , with a long way to fall. The shooting, the cutting, the music, the vibes, the colours, children - all gorgeous. I can recognize the lurching tone, and the disinterested bluster of Burton’s acting but I think it all suits the hokum just fine; the induced- somnambulism subplot renders cinema and spectatorship as rich and medium-specific subtext (De Palma did the exact same thing, the same year, in The Fury - another movie goofs laugh at instead of with). The recent doc is fine but suspended a bit awkwardly between advocacy and mockery; Boorman, and the devil both deserve better but rest assured Owen Glieberman’s smarmy, Monkey’s-Paulette review will not see heaven.

2026-10-24 The Cycle (2026) thejoshl primary 3.0 no This review may contain spoilers. Spends about an hour or so as a mostly mediocre slow-burn horror-procedural about a cold case forensics specialist and the bizarre discovery of her absent father's corpse at the scene of an unrelated crime, but when it (literally) transforms into its zombie-werewolf final form and starts tearing throats out and decimating police stations with all kinds of gooey, hairy practical FX it really delivers and even elevates some of what came before. The corpse waking up on the slab like the sloth guy in Se7en and going straight for the coroner's leg, then doing its American Werewolf transformation... just awesome stuff. Some of the gnarliest werewolf carnage since idk Dog Soldiers ? Honestly, even the emotional father-daughter stuff I wasn't completely sold on lands in the final stretch. Her watching the Empty Man suicide experiment tape he left behind, the foggy beach confrontation which does a much better job of ripping off the tragic climax of The Fly than another recent mainstream horror did. Reminded me of Zach Creggar's recent quote about how he starts and hardly finishes a lot of Shudder movies. Which he's not necessarily wrong to do so most of the time tbh, but if there was an argument to be made for why someone maybe shouldn't this would be the one.

2026-10-24 Paper Tiger (2026) silentdawn secondary 3.0 no 60 Chicago International Film Festival #9 Couldn't muster up a lot of enthusiasm for this, to be honest. James Gray once again returns to examine the tragedy of the middle class, failed upward mobility, thwarted masculinity and codes of honor (both professional and familial), with overcooked melodramatic heights and despairing, haunted lows. You'd think that heading back to an overt crime drama structure would put these ideas together in a more assured register, but so much of the run-time operates as a standardized 80s dad-epic. Not bad but certainly nothing to write home about. Adam Driver and Miles Teller both turn in sophisticated roles on two entirely different emotional planes, Scarlett Johansson less so, although that's more to do with the underwritten nature of the role. You'll be hearing plenty of noise about two key sequences in this and yes, they're as good as you've heard, even if the entire project left me feeling a twinge of diminishing returns towards the James Gray Auteurist Project and what exactly he has left to say as an artist.

2026-10-24 Tangles (2026) silentdawn secondary 4.5 no 85 Chicago International Film Festival #8 Accurate and true to life - not just in the broader details of Alzheimer's but also in the specificities that arise out of such a devastating diagnosis. All the family in-fighting, the late night baths, knives tossed in the kitchen pot, the loss of recognition and remembrance and the dull pain of a slow, ceaseless decline: these details are rendered with such care, and they're balanced alongside a queer story of great humor and nuance. A strong voice cast brings this animated memoir to the screen in a profound way. Be on the look out for this one.
