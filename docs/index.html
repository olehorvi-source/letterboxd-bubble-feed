2026-08-25 Lady Snowblood 2: Love Song of Vengeance (1974) thejoshl primary 3.0 yes With Lady Snowblood's personal revenge mission complete by the conclusion of the original film , Osada and Fujita take the undercurrent of corruption/nationalism/militarism woven throughout and develop it into the primary text. Having Meiko Kaji tasked with spying on a radical left-wing workers/anarchist movement brewing in the plague-ridden slums for a secret military police and realizing these guys are actually hunks who spend all day smoking and drinking, reading and writing about collectivism, and making love to their wives (and sucking on their toes… not going to go for the easy Tarantino joke). The result is a lot less limb-severing blood geyser swordplay (though still some!) and a lot more unpleasant political torture. Also, a lot more of an unfocused slow-burn narrative that sees Kaji sidelined a bit in favor of the two brothers attempting to resist their doomed oppression at the hands of a sadistic military official that is almost a Vincent Price in Witchfinder General / Masque of Red Death type figure. Looks beautiful though (that beach opening and sword sticking out of ocean title card!!), the visceral rage being so pointedly directed away from individual criminals and towards an entire brutal government system is a cool change, and the fiery finale that sees Kaji slice her way through a mansion (smashing through a two-way mirror and briefly wielding a shotgun!) while the slums are “cleansed” to a charred crisp, before taking up arms with her half-dead fever-stricken comrade and kneeling over a pile of bloody corpses with the Hinomaru and Rising Sun flags above her on either side… Good stuff! 2026-08-24 Lady Snowblood (1973) thejoshl primary 4.0 yes Nowhere near as intensely stylized as Ito's Female Prisoner movies which this was clearly greenlit in the wake of—Meiko Kaji as an avenging angel of death who sings her own theme song and goes on a revenge mission to graphically kill men/rapists? Sounds familiar! But credit where it's due, the foundation of her being a child deliberately conceived in prison in order to inherit her mother’s trauma and raised/trained from birth to be a "demon" with the blade and complete her vendetta mission from the grave is simply one of the great 70s revenge movie set-ups, and doled out here with some effective non-linear storytelling. And all the handheld Lone Wolf and Cub blood geyser splatter sequences of her cutting through bad guys limbs/necks like butter with the same killing machine ease Bruce Lee would have while clearing a room with his fists are just incredible. The masquerade ball finale and closing bloody image in the (you guessed it) snow are directly lifted by Tarantino for a reason; they're great! And lastly, this probably goes without saying: Meiko Kaji… That’s it. One of the greatest actresses to ever stare down the barrel of a camera. Feels like at any moment her eyes are going to pierce the screen and cut you up too for watching all her pain and suffering. Full discussion on ep 25 of my podcast SLEAZOIDS .
