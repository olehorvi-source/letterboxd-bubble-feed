2026-08-08 Bad Lieutenant: Port of Call – New Orleans (2009) thejoshl primary 4.0 yes Has very little connective tissue to the Ferrara original outside of also being about a ludicrously corrupt drug-addict cop who abuses his power and places sports bets. No junkie Catholic guilt soul-baring, anyway. In fact, as written by LA: Law 's William Finkelstein there's stretches that have a borderline DTV sequel vibe with time spent on a Law and Order plot about drug dealers exploiting/murdering immigrants, and a weirdly stacked ensemble of supporting character actors making brief but memorable appearances like TV episode guest cameos. And yet, Herzog and Cage's involvement changes, deranges and elevates everything. Herzog shoots these proceedings as just as much of a surreal, seemingly improvised handheld descent into madness as Aguirre , only instead of Kinski's Spanish conquistador he's following Cage's bizarre interpretation of a Dirty Harry style cop terrorizing post-Katrina New Orleans with his crotch magnum. Cage has never needed an excuse to go big and bizarre with a performance and yet to follow Keitel's animalistic anguish he opts to go back to some of that Vampire's Kiss / Face/Off mania, skulking around like a hunched Nosferatu, appearing wide-eyed behind doors mid-shave, waving his gun around like a lunatic, having bizarre stoned hallucinations, occasionally (and seemingly at random) saying all his lines in a scene like he's Jimmy Stewart doing an old gangster movie character impression and then dropping it a scene later. Herzog and Cage together creating what feels like this weird uncanny satire of an American crime movie, right down to the bizarre almost cheap art film look that at times had me thinking about The Return . Which now that I think about it could've used some alligator/iguana-vision, crack-smoking with Xzibit, and break-dancing souls. The sudden "everything's coming up Millhouse" fantasy wrap-up to the murder plot is so funny to compare to the bleak final shot of the original, but maybe there's something to be said about how these guys more often than not aren't killed but get away with it, get promoted and then get right back to it, and the final shot here still has its own uniquely strange poetry to it.
