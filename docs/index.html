2026-09-15 Bad Lieutenant: Tokyo (2026) thejoshl primary 3.0 no Found this simultaneously very watchable but frustrating at the same time. Miike essentially takes the first and last shot of Abel Ferrara's original Bad Lieutenant and fills out the in between with a conventional serial killer/yakuza thriller plot. Which is not a problem when he gets to indulge his signature highly stylized tastelessness: graphic sacrificial dismemberments, a nightclub fight scene that gets the characters literally horny, a crazy death during (and via) sex, multiple exploding heads during a car chase somehow. This stuff is his bread and butter, and a lot of it looks and sounds and moves well with real control over the mood and pace. Unfortunately, almost anytime a character opens their mouth you will wish it was doing just about anything else. Lily James in particular is given some pretty tough, thankless stuff to do and she tries to mostly accent her way through it. I also think you just can't spend so long atmospherically setting up a dual-serial killer hunt and resolve both in such easy, anti-climactic fashion. I get that that's something that both the other film's sort of do (the Keitel one with how it denies him the cathartic revenge against the rapists, the Cage one with how it tidies up his absurdist spiral of dirty work like a drugged-out fantasy/dream), but this one wraps up in a way that felt more like an afterthought shrug rather a deliberate choice. Liv Morgan gets a scene at the midpoint that's so good it's a legit shame she's essentially not involved in the finale at all. Cool last shot though.

2026-09-15 The Debut (2026) thejoshl primary 4.0 no That rare movie that is almost entirely a vessel for a performance and is actually good because... I mean, what a performance. Eisenberg is smart to keep his basic A Streetcar Named Marge set-up of a timid, neglected housewife turned confident, over-committed method actor relatively simple, and then just following the absurdity of it where it leads him. Starting in the smaller register of a sweet woman rousingly discovering an expressive and cathartic technique that have a real use and value for her years of pent-up frustrations, before having it comically tied to and descent into an escalating series of gags with their sights set on the power-tripping self-importance/obsession of actors and and the sheer lunacy (positive and negative) of trying to achieve "truth" and "reality" through artifice. It's a great running joke that that this all takes place in the low-stakes world of community theatre and yet Daniels deploys all the classic visual vocabulary of a backstage showbiz pressure-cooker melodrama (love the sudden warm spotlight touches too when she gets excited about something). It's an over-cranked intensity that works in tandem with Moore, who is so unreal good at playing this adult theatre kid version of her housewife from Safe completely straight, even as she takes her therapeutic sense of actorly "play" to some extremely darkly funny places. Whether that's remaining adorably, performatively sweet in the face of the casual cruelty she receives from her family, friends, and domineering theater director (Giamatti, also excellent, but in a more minor register than Moore despite his "I AM YOUR GOD" moment), or finding such immense, crowd-pleasing satisfaction from essentially LARPing to a progressively more dangerous and unhealthy degree. The details of which are better left unspoiled but some of the scenes of her alone in that dingy motel room had me losing my mind.
