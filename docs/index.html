2026-10-29 The Burning (1981) thejoshl primary 4.0 no If the first Friday the 13th movie was made by competent crafts people it would've look something like this. The straightforward, economical slow-burn naturalism is actually a feature not a bug here, and these kids often feel uncomfortably real enough that when Tom Savini is allowed to go beast mode on them (which he absolutely does with that raft massacre and mine shaft finale) it's actually effectively traumatizing and haunting in the ways these movies were probably supposed to be rather than campy. The Weinstein creep factor of it all is an undeniable knock against it, but it's at least marginally cancelled out by the delightfulness of confident baby Jason Alexander (who somehow still looks like he's in his 30s) and the banging synth score. Bonus points for not just opening and closing on fireball stunts but throwing in the black gloved fedora giallo bit too and the axe to the deformed monster face which clearly ended up being a test-run for the ending of The Final Chapter .

2026-10-28 Dao (2026) silentdawn secondary 4.5 no 89 Chicago International Film Festival #11 Long-form cinema productively torn between modern and ancestral modes of tradition. Flows at such a steady, unhurried pace of observation, through which the customs of heritage find commonality and contrast with our present-day rituals. Often reaches an ecstatic sustained energy even with the nearly 3.5 hour runtime, bursting at the seams with song and dance, ceremony and memorial. Even if this may feel a bit unassembled in certain stretches, some patience is worth the journey, especially when the two timelines begin to converge in ways that feel disruptive to its pre-established continuity. Rarely do docu-dramas feel so liberated in their ambition of dismantling reality and putting it back together again, but this movie does it. Electrifying!
