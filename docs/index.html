2026-10-24 Paper Tiger (2026) silentdawn secondary 3.0 no 60 Chicago International Film Festival #9 Couldn't muster up a lot of enthusiasm for this, to be honest. James Gray once again returns to examine the tragedy of the middle class, failed upward mobility, thwarted masculinity and codes of honor (both professional and familial), with overcooked melodramatic heights and despairing, haunted lows. You'd think that heading back to an overt crime drama structure would put these ideas together in a more assured register, but so much of the run-time operates as a standardized 80s dad-epic. Not bad but certainly nothing to write home about. Adam Driver and Miles Teller both turn in sophisticated roles on two entirely different emotional planes, Scarlett Johansson less so, although that's more to do with the underwritten nature of the role. You'll be hearing plenty of noise about two key sequences in this and yes, they're as good as you've heard, even if the entire project left me feeling a twinge of diminishing returns towards the James Gray Auteurist Project and what exactly he has left to say as an artist.

2026-10-24 The Animal (1976) brofromanother primary no The repeated motif of figures cresting a faraway hill, unmistakable yet indistinct, suits a film determined to keep itself and the viewer in the foreground of some deeper mystery. The idea of cinema as a physical, palpable space to be inhabited - with the individual film just passing through - links The Animal to any number of unexpectedly copacetic filmmakers and titles: I thought of James Benning and Kiarostami along with Herk Harvey.
