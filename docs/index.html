2026-09-22 Forgotten Island (2026) davidehrlich secondary 3.0 no There is a lot going on in Joel Crawford and Januel Mercado’s “Forgotten Island,” a heartfelt but exhaustingly hyper-frenetic Dreamworks Animation comedy that remixes “Superbad,” “Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind,” and “The Hangover” into a rave-like children’s adventure steeped in Filipino folklore. It’s a touching story of memory and friendship (one that sees the “How to Train Your Dragon” and “Kung Fu Panda” production studio doubling down on the raw emotionality that worked so well for “The Wild Robot”), and also a manic cartoon that goes out of its way to make a joke about Will Smith slapping Chris Rock at the Oscars (because Dreamworks is still the house that “Shrek” built, at the end of the day). It’s a heavily researched but kid-friendly glimpse into a culture that Hollywood has never drawn to life before, and also an anime-inspired piece of cultural syncretism that repeatedly incorporates actual footage from the Arnold Schwarzenegger classic “Predator.” Did I mention that Dave Franco voices a cool pubescent weredog — a combination of words that no human has ever typed before — who uncontrollably transforms into a tiny chihuahua whenever he gets too close to a stick? Like I said, it’s a lot. Sometimes — frequently — too much. But it’s hard to complain about “too much” at a time when most of the animated movies this country pumps out aren’t nearly enough. While “Forgotten Island” is relentless, distractable, and overstuffed to a degree that waters down the simple power of its message (and makes it difficult to understand the basic principles of its plot), none of its lovingly sourced ingredients ever lose the basic essence of their flavor. ~this review continues on IndieWire~
