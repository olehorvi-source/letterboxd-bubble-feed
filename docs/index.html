2026-09-13 Misty Green (2026) davidehrlich secondary 2.5 no A raw and biting — or at least vigorously gumming — showbiz character study about a faded Black actress whose entire adult life has been shaped by the peaks and valleys of living in a place that often seems flat on the surface, Chris Rock’s “Misty Green” is preoccupied with the relationship between feast and famine from the moment it starts, and by the Los Angeles-specific tendency for those two conditions to remain worlds apart even when they overlap. Even when they walk right past each other on Hollywood Blvd., or audition for the same role on a studio lot. Fortune is a fickle thing: One day you’re winning some award on TV, and the next day, a short 18 years later, you’re being evicted from your dead sugar daddy’s beachside pied-à-tierre because he left it to his wife in his will. That’s la la land for ya. As Rock takes pains to remind us in the photo montage that opens his latest and most serrated film (a film that shares its setting’s lack of subtlety), L.A. is a city where world-class museums are just down the block from Skid Row, where the Oscars are hosted inside of a hideous shopping mall, and where the paparazzi thoughtlessly climb over homeless people to snap photos of starlets like Lindsay Lohan (to name the first of several celebrities this movie catches with a random stray). How is anyone supposed to stop chasing the bag when it feels like, even at their lowest ebb, they’re still always close enough to reach out and grab it? By the same token, how is anyone supposed to have the bag when there’s almost no degree of success that can ensure they’ll let you keep it? If such a degree of success even exists, it’s certainly not available to a woman like Misty. Played to wearily ferocious perfection by “Slow Horses” powerhouse Rosalind Eleazar in what should be a star-making performance (but you never know in this town!), the almost 40-year-old Misty has developed a full script of excuses for the downward spiral she’s been on since winning an Emmy for a TV show called “Red Eye” in 2008. “Too Black to be with Leo, not Black enough to be with Damson, not a nepo baby or a Brit.” She lays it out with a battle-worn intensity that deflects attention from what she’s trying hard not to say; we think “it’s a funny meta-joke that Eleazar is a Brit,” and “is Damson Idris really famous enough to belong in that sentence?,” and not “what has Misty done for the last two decades besides making excuses for herself and getting cozy with some old guy who let her live in his spare apartment?” We get a partial answer to that question after she bumps into Jordan, the director whose “Cinderella” remake was going to launch Misty into the stratosphere until she got coked up out of control and punched him in the face on set (Rock plays the part himself, wise and resigned in a role that warmly reflects how the onetime firebrand has softened with age). She’s so deeply internalized Hollywood’s treatment of Black women that she can’t even give herself a break. So yeah, it’s true that Misty is a complicated person in a business that’s still painfully simple in its approach to race, age, and second chances, but it’s also true that her inestimable talents as an actress are equaled by her gift for self-sabotage. Alternately, and sometimes all at once, the movie is a straightforward gender comedy about 21st century dating, a high-concept “What Women Want” riff about a chauvinist who gets the tables turned on his penchant for objectification, a monkey’s paw morality play in which a hapless lad does “Obsession” to every creature on Earth with XX chromosomes (including dogs, insects, and even his own mother), and, last and kind of least, a half-interested ethnography about the relationship between social maturity and biological imperatives in the post-Apatowian manchild. 2026-09-12 Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother (2026) thejoshl primary 4.0 no Starting the nomination process now for Mahershala Ali's "Black ISIS" Muslim Samurai to join the canon of skilled killers trying to live by old warrior codes in this modern world; both of the hired hitman variety ( Ghost Dog , John Wick ) and father-protector variety ( Lone Wolf & Cub ). Shoutout to he and writer-director Bassam Tariq who clearly had some real passion behind what they wanted to do with their Blade movie at Marvel and when those morons let it fall apart found a way to channel that into a soulful character study of a man existentially wrestling with not just his faith, but what it means to be a traditional and devout (recently widowed) patriarch as well as a literal Sword of God, and whether that moral/spiritual contradiction is sustainable. The pathos of that idea is fairly obvious, but the minutia of how this goes about dramatically, atmospherically, and even comedically exploring it is consistently rich. Whether through amusing hypocrisy of his stern, conservative parenting style and attempt to shield his children from porn while the criminal contacts he takes jobs from produce it (and far worse "Haram ass activities"); or the legitimate logistical tension of him simply trying to navigate the proper channels to attain breast milk as a newly single father like with the same single-minded intensity he might for climbing a trafficking/drug network; or, finally, how his gift for gruesome mass murder begins to bleed into and infect his domestic life, his apartment complex, his mosque. Walking a satisfying line between delivering the bloody, athletic and cathartic revenge film fight setpieces that he gets into in various bathrooms (Ali's Casino Royale moment), neon strip clubs (mid conversion to southern churches?!), and Tony Montana ass mansions that is far better than anything that would've got by a Marvel executive. Yet doing so fully aware of the hypocrisy of its own excitement, and the never-ending collateral damage that will continue until the cycle is broken. It takes a lot of skill to make a movie like this and not only make it look and sound this good but get you to care so deeply about this man, his detailed relationship to his kids, and the complicated lessons about faith he's imparting to them via his contradictory words and actions, etc. That you don't even mind that it sets up a Rambo bloodbath against characters who absolutely deserve it (shoutout John Cho as the Southern born-again marine cult leader), only to choose grace and God over further pain and destruction, and doing so not in a way that feels cheap or sentimental, but poetic and mythic.
