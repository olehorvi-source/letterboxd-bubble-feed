2026-09-13 The Face of Horror (2026) thejoshl primary 4.0 no Anna Biller continues her project of playfully putting feminist gender war theory up against some of the most lovingly hand-crafted 60s analog fetish filmmaking pastiche you will ever witness. This time moving away from the realm of sexploitation and into pure gothic Hammer horror, with some Roger Corman (especially those Edgar Allen Poe adaptations) and Mario Bava thrown in for good measure. This one based on an old Kabuki female revenge/ghost story, repurposed to take place in medieval England and centered around a beautiful farmer's daughter whose performatively chivalrous knight husband is actually a misogynistic serial killing scoundrel. His monstrous military violence and total disregard of women barely disguised by his prince charming façade, and eventually resulting in a scheme to rid of himself of his wife (not before horribly disfiguring her!) and dooming himself to cathartically hellish punishment. Similarly to The Love Witch , this kind of requires you to be a sicko that enjoys even the shaggy, stilted flaws of this era, but if you can get on its wavelength there's real poetry and humor to how she goes about turning her obsession with all this over-stylized retro "beautiful gowns" lavishness and incorporates it into her mission to vengefully reclaim this iconography from an era where sexism was in more intensely plain sight. And thus could be dealt with in similarly broad genre strokes! It goes without saying that the deep focus 35mm lensing, colorful lighting, meticulous sets/costumes, red paint gore, and old-school dreaminess is drop-dead gorgeous, and at times probably luxuriated in for the sake of it. But this fixation on beautifully artificial, uncannily out-of-time surfaces also serves as a comically pointed contradiction to the rotten underlying medieval melodrama dynamic. Biller and her actors gleefully leaning into the campy over-acting, the ridiculous retrograde battle of the sexes dialogue, the systemic critique of how blatant and encouraged men's bad behavior is, and taking the central idea that a woman's value in this world stems the whether or not she can maintain her performative, objectifying beauty, and taking it to violently upsetting extremes. Rotting/charred corpses, decapitated heads, and protruding eyeballs galore, a Masque of Red Death -esque musical sequence about how death makes us all equal (rich-poor, men-women), a series of hallucinatory Black Sabbath Italian horror ghost-nightmare setpieces. Whether it works for you or not, no one else is making movies like this.
