2026-08-04 Peppermint Candy (1999) thejoshl primary 4.0 no A novelistic portrait of not just the tormented, suicidal man at its center but of an entire nation that feels traumatized and corrupted and futureless. Tracing the events that shaped him into the depressed, screaming mess (standing in front of a train waiting for it to hit him) in a Memento -ish reverse-chronological structure that slowly and painfully catalogs a series of bad decisions, material conditions and institutional pressures that transformed him from a bright, curious student with a passion for photography and in love with a girl, to a man capable of deeply upsetting military/police/domestic violence, and unable to make any sort of real emotional or romantic connection despite his efforts. Even before you get to the literal Gwangju Uprising stuff it's quite clear that the political/historical context of living under and collaborating with a right-wing martial law dictatorship was the poison that forever changed this generation of men (and the women that were unfortunate enough to come into their orbit) even before the economic crisis. And Lee Chang-dong's choice to start at the bleak ending and gradually sift backwards through 20-years accumulating details that bring the full picture into focus feels done less out of the sadistic misery some might accuse him of than a heart-wrenching desire to go back and change things. Something the cyclical rhyming ending almost suggests is possible. Now that we see the pattern/path, surely we won't make the same mistakes, right? Yet even in a fleeting moment of bliss in this memory of a specific time and place he declares in the opening scene he's going back to, the sound of that unstoppable train of time/progress rattles on. "Life is beautiful... Do you really think so?" A beautiful bummer.
