2026-11-08 Godzilla Minus Zero (2026) thejoshl primary 3.0 no This review may contain spoilers. I really respect Yamazaki's attempt to return this series back to its post-WWII roots, where images of Hiroshima were processed through the science-fiction horror metaphor of a giant mutated dinosaur visiting terrifying destruction upon Japanese cityscapes, overflowing hospitals, sending scientists and military officials into confused hysteria and heated moral and philosophical debates on weapons of war. Unfortunately, compared to Minus One , I think there was a sense of structural simplicity with how it went about conveying this idea and directly connecting its character work and action last time around that got a bit lost here—the 2nd half of that last one was functionally " Jaws but the shark is Godzilla". Awesome idea! The few setpieces here are still insanely impressive considering the budget level (that mid-film Ghidorah dogfight in particular is astonishing, despite his design kinda looking Game of Thrones -ish), but the sparseness of them is more felt this I think due to how unfocused and heavy-handed its attempts to expand the ensemble wartime melodrama are. A lot of the time it feels like characters and elements he invented to move the trajectory of the messy plotting along (a supernaturally gifted meteorologist who attempts to Jason Lives Ghidorah, the biologist who invents a The Birds machine to avenge his family, the kamikaze pilot's co-parent and her radiation poisoned "Godzilla Cells") were simply given such intensely sullen backstory and motivation because he gave it to the kamikaze pilot in the last film so now naturally everyone should have it, but also he's still around having it too. It's too much! The result is we spend so much time on obvious, teary-eyed melodrama of all these characters yelling at each other that ultimately amounts to a bunch of monotonous table-setting for a silly brawl, to the point that it often forgets to be an effective men-on-a-mission movie around it. The sentimentality is less the issue, as I honestly thought most of it was totally fine in the last one, and better than the human material we usually get in these films. The issue is how unnecessarily hard it presses some of those tearjerker buttons; all the orphanage stuff (the kid scream-crying in IMAX was so long and loud lol only to undo the decision the characters are making that results in it anyway?), the unpleasantness of the atomic bomb aftermaths were effective the first and maybe even second time but less so the ninth and tenth (the melting Manhattan vision bit pretty crazy tho). If the amount of time dedicated to this stuff was better I'd probably notice less that my buddy Godzilla is not really in this movie, but hey, when he does arrive, the gory beatdown with King G is pretty good I guess, it just felt weirdly divorced from the drama for me. Maybe there's just no clean way to be both the harrowing war movie and the silly wrestling ones of these at the same time. Nice try though.
