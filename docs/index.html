2026-09-25 Idiots (2026) thejoshl primary 3.0 no Nice to see Macon Blair back in indie dirtbag crime movie mode, though this one is more of a sad slacker buddy road movie than proper thriller. Something that I wasn't sure totally (and tonally) worked for me in the silly early goings of the two unemployed fuck-ups taking their freelance gig to drive a delinquent rich kid psycho to rehab, but was won over when it began to detour and really step on the gas (cue explosive shitting/puking), and revealed itself to be on a weirder, uglier path. It has the dingy, stained Georgia backroads texture, slapstick crime-gone-wrong plotting, and amusingly eccentric personalities/criminals they run into along the way you probably expect, but its farce has a layer of unpleasantness to it that overwhelms it, and simmers into an undercurrent of genuinely depressed portraiture of lives on the margins. Probably didn't need the line about the kid potentially being president one day to see what Blair is getting at, how little consequences are felt by him vs. how every other poor/working-class character around him experiences some form of desperation and degradation (and material/bodily harm) got the point across well enough. Honestly, I thought the whole cast was pretty good (even Shipka as the Russian stripper who I will forever have a hard time disassociating from Sally Draper and Braun who gets dangerously close to doing too much with his Soundcloud rapper "Pricka Bush Da Werewoof"), but Franco is the standout. He takes that smug grin and juvenile broad comedy boyishness he's got locked down to some pathetic and unhinged places.

2026-09-25 The Devil at Your Heels (1981) thejoshl primary 4.0 no Proud to live in a country where our tax dollars go towards sending a film crew to follow guys around who are essentially the real-life Hot Rod and Trailer Park Boys . As far as cinematic ego-driven death-drive obsessives are concerned, 70s Canadian stunt driver Ken Carter is one of the sweeter and more likeable precisely because the dream he yearns for seems so small and silly and yet still incredibly technically arduous and dangerous. The sheer passionate idiocy on display as he smashes his body to pieces to briefly make metal fly for some local Ontario dads and their kids is beautiful. How aware the documentary filmmakers are that what they're filming is stupid and insane and more than likely suicidal makes all the interviews they get even funnier and the stunt photography even scarier. The last-minute betrayal and anti-climactic conclusion to the failed 5-year journey to putting this mile-long Canada/US border stunt spectacle on is the kind of thing you can't script, it only happens when real idiots control their destiny. Pointed at the screen when Kenny Powers enters the picture down the stretch—you think Danny McBride has seen this? All the 70s Canadian dad outfits (and accents) should be put in a museum.
