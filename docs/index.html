2026-10-17 Carrie (2026) brofromanother primary no Back on Haunting of Hill House, it felt like Mike Flanagan was thinking actively about what it means to occupy an enduring narrative space, and how to renovate its architecture without vandalizing it. Now he’s a custodian of intellectual property, dutiful and dull. There’s a structural move off the top here by which he distinguishes his version of Carrie from its screen predecessors, and also from which it never recovers, and it’s not even really about the execution. The faithfulness to King across the episodes I’ve watched so far is mostly a matter of sprawl - of portent laid on thick and psychodrama stretched thin (not unlike the problems with The Shards, which is probably a worse showon every level, but more fun). The departure from De Palma, while understandable in the context of a miniseries adaptation, is also a farewell to anything like style, shape or sensibility. Don’t know if I’ll bother finishing the show.

2026-10-16 Nowhere to Lay My Eyes (2026) silentdawn secondary 4.0 no 73 Chicago International Film Festival #1 Was fully locked into the usual Hong sang-soo dinners and dynamics but the unexpected thrills came quick and early, starting with a pratfall (!!!) and diverting even further from the narrative necessities of what he ends up abstracting anyway. There is a lot to mine out of this 72 minute exercise - the pleasure found in art and creativity, the melancholy of life and all its practicalities, not to mention its self-aware insistence of our own existence being elusive, truncated, and just too damn precious to have this thing carry out any longer. Hong fans will eat this up, and I look forward to returning to it once I finally get off my ass and check off the heavy hitters in his filmography. A great (and apt) start to a festival in the windy city.
