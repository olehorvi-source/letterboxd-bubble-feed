2026-10-02 Digger (2026) silentdawn secondary 5.0 no 100 It's quite apt, in an apocalyptic sense, that Digger is the last major film released by Warner Bros, with the trades reporting a finalization of the conglomerate merger with Paramount on October 6th. On the precipice of oblivion (Skydance) is a last-supper mega-budget folly of art and creativity, hypocrisy and ignorance, not to mention the burden of reconciling and understanding our new normal. Huge movie for all the Doomers out there. It's also a top-shelf masterpiece, with extravagant detours and aesthetic ingenuity that is clashing hard - productively so - with its own broader satirical aspirations. Its zany depiction of political theatre and petty billionaire squabbles grated on my nerves in tantalizing fashion, often right before another narrative zig or zag that took my expectations to a whole other level. Digger is a film that is concerned with America's crumbling empire (and Europe's own complicity), and is determined to show the frayed pageantry exactly as it is, and it's unconcerned with merely satisfying the bland virtue signaling appetites of the Don't Look Up sensibility. Tom Cruise is nothing less than a caricature of Mr. Movies here - inflated ego, denial of responsibility, holding court and turning the tide while everyone else drowns. He is my turns maniacal, curt, schlubby and grotesque. But that's only one part of him. Saying any more will be a disservice to where this movie goes, the surprises it has up its sleeve. Alejandro González Iñárritu's usual preconceptions of theatricality and magical realism find a daring match with Cruise's already endless layers of artifice and superstardom. It's an astonishing performance, knowingly pompous in a way that barely conceals a vulnerability not seen from Cruise since his 90s run. Supporting ensemble is absolutely stacked, too. And all lensed by Chivo in Vistavision? Unreal. Tremendous images and sound. Watching this movie felt like a high-wire circus act from every cast and crew member, and they're all operating at the peak of their powers. There are a flurry of reasons as to why this movie isn't doing well. It's abrasive and more than a bit annoying and the marketing is (deliberately) misleading. But the critical pitchforks that came out for it seem predicated on wilful misreads of the material. This is not a "hate-letter to cinema", this is not like Megalopolis (?) or Joker 2 (?!?) - this is a movie about the endurance of handcrafted art and collaboration at a moment in time where it is constantly, consistently under attack. Just look how futile it is. And how precious. That's what makes this movie important - it yearns for the audience to reckon with the fragility within ourselves. See it in IMAX before it gets booted off for only making ten whole dollars at the box office. You may not like the movie, but you won't regret the experience.
