2026-09-30 Verity (2026) davidehrlich secondary 3.0 no “It Ends with Us” author Colleen Hoover isn’t typically in the business of making women look bad (male writers have all but cornered that particular market since before the invention of the printing press), which is why it’s so curious that the latest movie adapted from her books seems determined to convince us that its title character — a grieving mother who’s been rendered catatonic by a recent car crash, and now requires full-time care from the stern Ukrainian nurse who tube-feeds her in between diaper changes — is actually a demon bitch from hell. Anne Hathaway would never! … or would she? Faithfully adapted from Hoover’s 2018 bestseller of the same name, Michael Showalter’s “Verity” is a ludicrous psycho-thriller as cold and tart as the wine cocktail that should come free with every ticket. It’s also a film whose enjoyably contrived pleasures, like those of its source material, hinge upon the fact that people are eager for any excuse to look down on a successful woman. And while men are eternally GOATed in that regard, women are just as capable of falling into that trap — especially when they feel that another woman’s success came at the expense of their own. Human nature! Gotta love it. Indeed, it’s “local author” Lowen Asleigh (Dakota Johnson) who scoffs at the idea of ghostwriting the next installment in Verity Crawford’s ultra-popular book series. It’s Lowen who rolls her eyes at the kind of airport paperbacks that have since made Hoover into a millionaire several times over, and Lowen who grows addicted to the unfinished autobiography that she finds on a USB stick inside a locked drawer of Verity’s writing desk — a self-excoriating but utterly remorseless memoir in which Verity outs herself as one of the book world’s biggest “monsters” since the days of Cathy Ames. But… we get it. I mean, of course we feel bad that Verity’s young daughter was killed in a canoe accident, even though Nick Antosca’s script reduces the scale of the tragedy as it’s presented in the novel, but the part of our brains that’s been corrupted by true-crime (in addition to centuries of misogynistic suspicion) can’t help but look sideways at the mom for surviving the same event.
