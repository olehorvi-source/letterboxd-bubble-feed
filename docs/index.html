2026-11-20 Is God Is (2026) thejoshl primary 3.0 no Cool to see a playwright adapt their own work for the screen and adjust so naturally to the difference between stage theatricality and cinematic theatricality. There is certainly a lot of dialogue that give the origins away, and I did find some of the attempts at eccentricity and poetry a little strained. Luckily it mostly overcomes that by clearly coming from a place of personal passion and being so filled with energetic stylistic flourishes that harken back to southern gothics, buddy road movies, and revenge movies. Especially Kill Bill with its episodic mission structure, Spaghetti Western twangs, flashbacks, etc. I mean Vivica A. Fox is even playing their mother! I wish it more fully committed to the violent revenge-is-ugly collateral damage/moral complications it occasionally briefly flirts with on their "mission from God" journey to kill their destructive patriarch, but ultimately Young and Johnson are great as the two leads/twins (and so is Sterling K. Brown as their "daddy" when they finally get to him), it has some really nice cinematography by Alexander Dynan ( First Reformed , The Card Counter), and some fun soundtrack choices. Wasn't expecting that Death Grips needle drop.

2026-11-20 The Weight (2026) thejoshl primary 3.0 no Really solid directorial debut. Decidedly old-school in setting and style, combining elements of Depression-era chain gang prison dramas, westerns, survival-thrillers, men-on-a-mission movies, etc. The procedural situation itself of desperate laborers mucking it through the wilderness on a potential suicide mission with gold on their backs brings to mind The Wages of Fear and Treasure of Sierra Madre for sure, but its got a few tricks of its own in terms of rendering the tense, gruff arduousness of this work and the power collaboration/solidarity with your fellow man. Highlights: the rickety rope bridge brick-throwing, the log jam river near-drowning, and the Southern Comfort boat stealing that turns into a stormy middle-of-the-night smothering. Having such a hopeful ending kinda surprised me and felt a little sudden/clean after milking such bleak suspense for so long, but even if I wished it hit me a bit a harder down the stretch there was still plenty to like here: some moody and effective editing choices, a great ambient score (featuring elements of acoustic, electronic and rock sounds), and Hawke is as per usual terrific.
