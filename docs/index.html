2026-10-15 Nathan for You: Finding Frances (2017) thejoshl primary 4.0 yes Somehow never logged this back in 2017 but rewatched in preparation for You Can See Everything since this seemed to be the turning point towards Fielder's more recent interests— The Rehearsal especially, which is my favorite thing he's done thus far. Leaning away from the "mean-funny" satire of reality TV/consumer capitalism baked into his absurd marketing schemes and more into the elaborate simulations of awkward, lonely people trying to make connections with each other. The inherent artificiality of the camera + deadpan performance art of his entire reality-bending project function in this finale as less of a gag (though still funny, the Mud 2 stuff is so good) and more of a vessel in which to explore the performative nature of how we go about finding reality/truth and coping with intense feelings. Watching Nathan go so off-book and allowing for such uncontrolled spontaneity as he follows this guy around (including keeping some of thornier aspects in, including his sexism/politics, etc) is fascinating on its own, and even more so how it all ends up clarifying and questioning his literal experiments with transactional companionship. Professional fakers sometimes yearn for real love too.

2026-10-15 Eaten Alive (1976) thejoshl primary 4.0 yes Tobe Hooper's underrated follow-up to Texas Chainsaw Massacre which takes on another psychotic redneck serial killer, but this time based on a real-life Texas one who allegedly fed his victims to his swamp alligator and realized with a sense of style that abandons the naturally gritty on-location borderline snuff realism for psychedelically artificial soundstages. Hooper essentially expands on and tries to sustain the more surreal, sweaty, screaming sensory assault finale of TCM for an entire movie and ends up with something closer to an Italian horror sexploitation Psycho . Complete with a deranged Southern bayou motel proprietor Judd (played by Old Hollywood noir actor Neville Brand) murdering a young, on-the-run sex worker who you briefly think might be the protagonist, and then setting his sights on a husband-and-wife (William Finley and Marilyn Burns) who book the next room. On a character/performance/story level you spend most of this in a state of hysterical confusion that suits the unnatural formal choices and glitchy score interspersed throughout all the country-western radio bangers. Effectively doing to your eyes and brain what it's doing to its characters: dropping you into strange, abstract, colorful fog machine swamp that's driving everyone into primal madness and consuming them. Young Robert Englund shows up briefly as the rapist Buck who's "rarin' to fuck" (Tarantino... brother...), the deep Argento red exterior lighting during the scythe kill/dog munching is gorgeous, and all the surreal sex-and-death that follows is unpleasant and hellish as it is in the best Giallos/slashers.
