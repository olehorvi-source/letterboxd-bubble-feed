This is a vast canvas of anguish that still nonetheless functions as a filmic object of tidy respectability. Conjures up plenty of stirring images with grandeur to spare even if every other line in the screenplay is seemingly underlined with neon-pink highlighter. A movie that is desperate to be recognized and consumed rather than more inwardly felt and reckoned with, which unfortunately causes a lack of flow and poetry in its segmented timeline, with the emotional beats getting lost in the shuffle of its own construction. Quite an erratic cocktail that includes Beau Travail , Late-90s/early-aughts Miramax-distributed historical epic, and mid-budget Termite Art character study with White Elephant aspirations (ala The Brutalist ). Not without its merits but never fully achieves lift-off. Then again, everyone else is going absolutely gaga over this so what do I know?

RIP to Nightmare on Elm Street 3 and The Blob legend Chuck Russell, this was my biggest blind spot in his filmography so I couldn't turn down a chance to see some beautiful 90s action trash. The witness protection/hi-tech arms deal paranoid conspiracy set-up (courtesy of The Wild Bunch and Sorcerer co-screenwriter Walon Green + anuncredited John Milius/Frank Darabont??) is as 90s cable techno-thriller as it gets, yet the macho presence of Arnold (and Terminator / Terminator 2 collaborators he brought with him, including cinematographer Adam Greenberg and uncredited screenwriter William Wisher) certainly gives it some of that 80s brand of action sci-fi maximalism too. The film occasionally gets awkwardly lost between those two eras and tones and is a bit overlong and more than bit stupid, but Russell for the most part keeps things fun and fluid and does a perfectly fine journeyman studio action-thriller job. CDs, rail-guns, mid-air plane shootouts (lost it at beat where he goes Point Break mode and they turn the plane around lmao), dodgy CG gator massacres—happy flat fuck Friday to all who celebrate. For some reason the supporting cast is filled with an incredible collection of James C's (Caan, Coburn, Cromwell), and Alan Silvestri is going crazy on the flutes and guitar solos. Waited the whole movie for Arnold to drop the “erase” pun/one-liner and it didn’t disappoint. [ 35mm ]
