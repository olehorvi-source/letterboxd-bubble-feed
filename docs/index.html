2026-09-11 Bucking Fastard (2026) thejoshl primary 4.0 no Two chicks at the same time, man... An extremely funny and sad movie about a pair of eccentric, attached-at-the-hip Dead Ringers sisters who are very strange and out of step with/rejected by "normal" society until suddenly, just when you think there's no way Herzog could possibly get some Malickian photographed nature/animal/cave stuff into this story, it is an extremely lyrical and moving movie about those sisters' tunnel-digging search for a better (dream) world where they can be free. The Mara's are excellent (every bit of overlapping dialogue and body language expertly controlled) but it was the dedication to David Lynch that kinda unlocked this for me. Whose weirdness allowed them to uncover the enormity and beauty of the world more than his?

2026-09-11 River (2026) thejoshl primary 4.0 no The Daddy Issues sibling melodrama set-up for this is a bit generic but once this gets its killing machine engine revved-up, this as relentless and gnarly of an old-school stalk-and-prey slasher as I’ve seen in some time. Part Craven ( The Rivers Have Eyes ?), part The Strangers , part survival/home-invasion thriller, with a really good eye for escalating its various cat-and-mouse logistics. Simple and economical stuff but impressively constructed for a debut. Great geographical use of the locations (the boat and the house); well-composed, naturally lit frames (by moonlight, flashlights, flares, fire, etc); some creative use of slow-motion and dissolves that generate this creepy, contextless sense of the mythic, supernatural inevitability hunting them down in between the more conventional physical violence and jump scares. Levy and Rothe are both well-versed enough final girls to sell that quality with their eyes and screams too. Crossbow yank and beer chug of the year, hands down.
2026-09-11 Club Kid (2026) thejoshl primary 4.0 no "Is this a good cry or a bad cry?" "I don't know." Really beautiful and immersive depiction of the queer NYC nightlife scene inside a Sundance dramedy about a party/drug-addict who realizes he should probably go back to school and get a job once adulthood is suddenly thrust upon him in the form of a cute kid with good taste in music. Part of me felt this was a little too surprisingly easy and frictionless as an actual father-son drama for a good part of its runtime, though honestly that was mostly made up for by how comically "untraditional" the details of said relationship and environment were. (Lost it at the "Fucking bitch!! You're gonna let him talk like that?!" bit.) Before realizing that that was the point being made here, and it planned on dropping the hammer the entire time. So congrats to everyone involved, I teared up exactly on command, and while doing so realized there's definitely something a lot sneakier and more interesting about how Firstman is using the conventions of a formulaic "universal" story than I at first thought. Especially with how those beats dynamically interact with the broader community portraiture, which is both completely clear-eyed about all the sex and drugs, but also loving and non-judgmental about it, and keenly aware of its existence as an alternative to the "traditional" family structures queer people are often denied or abused within/running away from. Really moving stuff.
2026-09-11 Wild Horse Nine (2026) thejoshl primary 3.0 no Was kinda hoping to have more to say about this one but it's quite literally just In Bruges again but they're 70s CIA black ops guys and they travel to Easter Island. I love that movie and how well it deploys its crassly juvenile hangout buddy comedy stuff as a mask for its regretful, self-loathing, Catholic confessional purgatory. So ultimately I had a good time and laughed a lot at Rockwell, Malkovich (and Buscemi, in the Fiennes role) putting their American boomer neo-western/CIA conspiracy spin on it in the details. (Especially the big payoff for the JFK stuff doled out throughout.) But despite the change in dress-up and location, it honestly felt a little weird and desperate after a while that it was adhering so closely to the exact same beats and even jokes. If I want to revisit a movie where a bunch of farcical and gruesome death/destruction follows John Malkovich's attempt to write a memoir I'll likely stick with Burn After Reading .
