2026-08-14 American Doctor (2026) davidehrlich secondary 4.0 yes Like millions upon millions of people around the world, including a much higher percentage of the Jewish population than Zionists would ever care to admit, I have been haunted by the profound helplessness I feel in response to the ongoing genocide that Israel is committing against the Palestinian people. I’ve protested at length, I’ve been extremely outspoken about the slaughter online, I’ve raised aid money for the children of Gaza, and I’ve watched so many of my peers do just as much, if not a whole lot more, in an effort to confront one of the worst and most unapologetic humanitarian atrocities since the Holocaust — an atrocity committed in my name, and paid for with my tax dollars. And to what end? There are no moral victories in an ethnic cleansing. It’s estimated that Israel has killed more than 1,200 Palestinians since the “ceasefire” went into effect last October, and that is only the official count. One day, sooner than you might think, everyone will indeed have always been against this, but that will be of little consolation to the dead, just as it will be of little comfort to those of us who failed to stop them from dying. Poh Si Teng’s bracing, tortured, and utterly vital “American Doctor” is a documentary born from — and in constant dialogue with — that gnawing sense of helplessness. Directed by a disillusioned veteran of America’s media industrial complex (Teng quit her job at ABC News in order to finance this project with $150,000 of her own money), it’s a film that powerfully, if counterintuitively, addresses that helplessness by looking at it through the lens of the most heroic and capable people among us: a trio of medical practitioners who travel to Gaza in order to provide their services to patients at the embattled Nasser Medical Complex. Each of these men is willing and able to save Palestinian lives with their bare hands, even if that means traveling to a place where children are murdered at 600 times the rate in Ukraine, and doctors are targeted with similar impunity. Likewise, and in spite of their extraordinary abilities, each of these men is also wrestling with their individual limitations in the face of such institutional depravity. Teng’s film — a moving patchwork that was shot by Gaza-based cinematographers Ibrahim Al Otla and Christopher Renteria, directed from New York City, and palpably shaped by practical limitations of its own — harnesses its subjects’ frustrations as much as it salutes their valor. The result is a documentary that implicitly honors these incredible doctors, but more acutely leverages their plight to address the numbing exasperation that such an atrocity is designed to instill among otherwise good people. It’s a documentary premised on the idea that nobody can stop the madness on their own, and how that’s all the more reason for everybody to resist it on their own terms. “American Doctor” is able to broach the genocide from that angle because, for all of its barbed criticism of the global news media, the film never presumes to know the best way of cutting through the noise. On the contrary, its unformed shape and melange of styles add to the sense that Teng is actively searching for the answer to that question with every cut. ~this review continues on IndieWire~
