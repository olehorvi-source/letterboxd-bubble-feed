2026-10-26 Jason Goes to Hell: The Final Friday (1993) thejoshl primary 3.0 no Have to say, despite the fact that this an obviously extremely flawed movie (I mean even just technically it was clearly a debut by a recent film school graduate in over his head), I can’t lie, after two entries in this franchise that felt like they were progressively descending into tame formulaic comedies, it was nice to get a genuinely weird, disgusting and deranged horror movie. I won’t argue much with the common critiques—the new Jason bloodline mythology is stupid, only 10 minutes of (ugly, bloated, mutated) Jason or whatever is wack, would’ve been way cooler if this was actually a Jason adventure movie in hell which it is decidedly not, the score is chintzy as hell, at times it looks like a 90s TV/DTV movie, etc. But viewed through the very specific lens that this is Sam Raimi’s SFX/gore/creature team doing more of a sequel to Jack Sholder’s body-swapping body-horror sci-fi chase-thriller The Hidden inside of a “Friday the 13th” movie (in a way not dissimilar to how Halloween 3 is its own strange Michael Myers-less conspiracy horror film) I was able to pretty quickly get over any disappointment about that, adjust my expectations and go along with all the gross, mean, cartoonish nonsense. Right from the cold open subversion of Jason being honey-potted by a supermodel FBI agent pretending to be a nubile teen rolling up to a Crystal Lake cabin all alone only for Jason to be hit with floodlights, a barrage of slow-motion machine gun fire, and then exploded to bloody, charred pieces I was excited by the unpredictability of where this one was going to go next. Heart-eating coroners, giant worm/larvae tongues, hogtied shaving (??), slimy Hellraiser body melting/decomposing, Terminator police station/diner assaults (it’s their fault for selling 2-for-1 Voorhees burgers with a side of Jason fries!), magic Evil Dead daggers, worm facehugger Jason trying to kiss a baby and then rebirthing himself by crawling into the vagina of his half-sisters corpse, before ultimately being Drag Me To Hell ’d… And that’s before you even get into the bizarre (at times homoerotic) character dynamics like whatever is going on between the dorky lead and his deputy buddy, the most evil yuppie TV field reporter in movie history, the black bounty-hunting cowboy “Creighton Duke” (Steven Williams attempting his best Quint from Jaws impression) who knows a lot about this new moronic Jason lore for mysteriously unclear reasons and reveals it at the expense of breaking fingers (which he seems to get horny over??) Perhaps helped that my first experience with this was the new 4K Arrow release of the unrated cut too. The gore FX here are easily the best outside of Savini’s; that graphic tent cowgirl sex scene that ends in blood-spurting impalement/torso tearing is legitimately disgusting. 2026-10-25 Exorcist II: The Heretic (1977) brofromanother primary no The standard cinephile line is that Ex 2 functions as a corrective to its predecessor - that Boorman’s earnest, flailing folly stands in opposition to Friedkin’s savvy, machine-tooled brutality. There’s something to this insofar as Boorman more or less went on the record about his against-the-grain intentions, while Friedkin registered his displeasure with the results in the same mocking terms as the majority of film critics descending, Pazazu-like, on a DOA sequel: “the worst piece of shit I’ve ever seen..a fucking disgrace… your mother sucks cocks in hell, Boorman.” Me, I’m built different. I like both movies, and also Blatty’s weird and wacky Exorcist III - itself a departure, heralding a decade and change of demonic urban- inferno procedurals (Seven, Cure, etc). My fondness for The Heretic is bound up mostly in its aesthetics, which are - as per authorities from Pauline Kael to Martin Scorsese - flatly stunning, though it’s worth noting that they often honour and extrapolate on the source material: the tug of war over Regan’s heart echoes the shot of her in the original reaching out while the statue of Pazazu lurks behind her. The sleepwalking sequence atop the skyscraper is so beautiful: modernism refracted and made uncanny , with a long way to fall. The shooting, the cutting, the music, the vibes, the colours, children - all gorgeous. I can recognize the lurching tone, and the disinterested bluster of Burton’s acting but I think it all suits the hokum just fine; the induced- somnambulism subplot renders cinema and spectatorship as rich and medium-specific subtext (De Palma did the exact same thing, the same year, in The Fury - another movie goofs laugh at instead of with). The recent doc is fine but suspended a bit awkwardly between advocacy and mockery; Boorman, and the devil both deserve better but rest assured Owen Glieberman’s smarmy, Monkey’s-Paulette review will not see heaven.
