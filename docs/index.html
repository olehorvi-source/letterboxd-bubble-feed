2026-08-05 The Samurai and the Prisoner (2026) thejoshl primary 4.0 no This review may contain spoilers. Always cool to see a modern master get to flex and play around in a classical form they clearly love, in this case the expertly calibrated ambient dread of Kurosawa’s horror films traded in for a patiently engrossing wartime jidaigeki straight out of the 50s-60s. As much as he maintains a sense of tradition in terms of look, rhythm, style, etc. The novel by Honobu Yonezawa he is adapting does have a unique and modern entry point to the setting though. By taking the real-life mystery surrounding warlord Araki Murashige’s (a fantastic Masahiro Motoki) decision to suddenly rebel against his brutal superior Oda Nobunaga, face besiegement for over year, and eventually flee and live the rest of his life in relative obscurity as a tea master, and coming up with a fictional theory on what he might have been reckoning with inside his castle walls during that time in his life: a series of contained murder mysteries that compound the sense of impending doom he and his men already face. It’s a fantastic idea, one that lends itself to claustrophobic mood, deceptive character psychology, and the satisfying procedural thrill of an unconventional genre archetype (a samurai) assuming the role of detective, coming across another corpse found in a puzzling state and working through the bizarre potential method and motive, and even having our samurai gather everyone around to hear his logical Columbo explanation for each one. An impossible missing arrow shot, a changed/distorted decapitated trophy head, a priest and his skilled protector seemingly supernaturally killed mid secret mission, and a proud warrior literally stuck down by lightning (and bullets). The two and half hours goes by quickly due to how well Kurosawa stages these events, blocks these actors, and maintains a consistent sense of distrust and manipulation you are attuned to even during seemingly innocuous conversations. The sneaky power of the film is the larger spiritual weight it gradually accumulates through those conversations. By maintaining a quiet but meticulously constructed stateliness and narrative genre logic he almost performs a sleight-of-hand trick on you with regard to what this movie actually is. Because it is indeed a murder mystery and satisfying to watch as one, but as the bodies pile up, as the resolution of each one only results in another question of the larger how/why of these events, you begin to realize there’s never really a proper catharsis to their resolution. They are simply an on-the-ground symptom of the irresolvable moral conflict of the irrational war games being played up top. An ambiguous atmosphere of senseless suffering and misery (that horrific flashback to Oda’s treatment of women hostages…) that Araki Murashige is at worst complicit in, at best helpless to prevent with his own logic. (Or that of his imprisoned enemy strategist Kanbei, played by an equally fantastic Masaki Suda who at times recalls Masato Hagiwara’s great work as Mamiya in Cure while locked in his dungeon and constantly interrogated and sought after for intelligent council.) What we are ultimately witnessing is a feedback loop of the chaos, uncertainty and “divine punishment” spread by men of power who believe in aggression and violence as the primary tool of a great leader. “Advance to paradise, retreat to hell” is the recurring proverb stated by multiple characters in the film, one of forward momentum at all costs. What Kurosawa suggests is that by going backwards, not only are you not being dishonorable or cowardly (as Araki Murashige is commonly thought of), you are being sensible, logical. Dying pointlessly and brutally in the middle of the night for some guy who will probably eventually betray you and execute your family… It’s got nothing on opting out and living a long life enjoying the pleasures/treasures of the world. Say, with a loved one, or a great cup of tea. Might make for a fun pairing with Nolan's The Odyssey about a guy who, haunted by his contributions to the war machine, chooses exile.
