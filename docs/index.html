2026-09-12 Everybody Wants to F*ck Me (2026) davidehrlich secondary 2.5 no Much like the soft boi archetype that it takes behind the woodshed for a dirty little beating, Jonathan Schey’s “Everybody Wants to Fuck Me” — its amusing title presented on screen without the asterisk — presents itself as a thoughtful and complex piece of work. Alternately, and sometimes all at once, the movie is a straightforward gender comedy about 21st century dating, a high-concept “What Women Want” riff about a chauvinist who gets the tables turned on his penchant for objectification, a monkey’s paw morality play in which a hapless lad does “Obsession” to every creature on Earth with XX chromosomes (including dogs, insects, and even his own mother), and, last and kind of least, a half-interested ethnography about the relationship between social maturity and biological imperatives in the post-Apatowian manchild. Alas, much like its manwhore protagonist, Schey’s debut feature is — for all of its postmodern fun — ultimately a lot more base and basic than it presents on the surface. At the end of the day, it just wants to get you into bed with a nice smile, a few irresistible references (the guy has a “Cleo from 5 to 7” poster in his bedroom and a pup named after Alana Haim!), and some empty promises of follow-through so that you’ll fall under the spell of its DCP for a night. The moment you start to get invested, this movie is already thinking about its next conquest; it flits between genres with the same cynicism that its hero wears a MUBI tote bag and “reads” Ottessa Moshfegh novels at his local cafe as bait, compulsively performing what it thinks we want to see in order to mask its (valid) fear that it doesn’t have anything of value to show us. At least that’s one way of demystifying why its protagonist would sooner spend a night with half the women in London than sleep with any of them twice, but at no point in “Everybody Wants to Fuck Me” does Adam Samuels — played by a very game Taron Egerton, his performance impressively sympathetic even at its most shit-eating and vice-versa — make a serious attempt to understand why he’s so addicted to fucking them. ~this review continues on IndieWire~
2026-09-12 Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother (2026) thejoshl primary 4.0 no Starting the nomination process now for Mahershala Ali's "Black ISIS" Muslim Samurai to join the canon of skilled killers trying to live by old warrior codes in this modern world; both of the hired hitman variety ( Ghost Dog , John Wick ) and father-protector variety ( Lone Wolf & Cub ). Shoutout to he and writer-director Bassam Tariq who clearly had some real passion behind what they wanted to do with their Blade movie at Marvel and when those morons let it fall apart found a way to channel that into a soulful character study of a man existentially wrestling with not just his faith, but what it means to be a traditional and devout (recently widowed) patriarch as well as a literal Sword of God, and whether that moral/spiritual contradiction is sustainable. The pathos of that idea is fairly obvious, but the minutia of how this goes about dramatically, atmospherically, and even comedically exploring it is consistently rich. Whether through amusing hypocrisy of his stern, conservative parenting style and attempt to shield his children from porn while the criminal contacts he takes jobs from produce it (and far worse "Haram ass activities"); or the legitimate logistical tension of him simply trying to navigate the proper channels to attain breast milk as a newly single father like with the same single-minded intensity he might for climbing a trafficking/drug network; or, finally, how his gift for gruesome mass murder begins to bleed into and infect his domestic life, his apartment complex, his mosque. Walking a satisfying line between delivering the bloody, athletic and cathartic revenge film fight setpieces that he gets into in various bathrooms (Ali's Casino Royale moment), neon strip clubs (mid conversion to southern churches?!), and Tony Montana ass mansions that is far better than anything that would've got by a Marvel executive. Yet doing so fully aware of the hypocrisy of its own excitement, and the never-ending collateral damage that will continue until the cycle is broken. It takes a lot of skill to make a movie like this and not only make it look and sound this good but get you to care so deeply about this man, his detailed relationship to his kids, and the complicated lessons about faith he's imparting to them via his contradictory words and actions, etc. That you don't even mind that it sets up a Rambo bloodbath against characters who absolutely deserve it (shoutout John Cho as the Southern born-again marine cult leader), only to choose grace and God over further pain and destruction, and doing so not in a way that feels cheap or sentimental, but poetic and mythic. "The whole world is a mosque."

2026-09-11 Bucking Fastard (2026) thejoshl primary 4.0 no Two chicks at the same time, man... An extremely funny and sad movie about a pair of eccentric, attached-at-the-hip Dead Ringers sisters who are very strange and out of step with/rejected by "normal" society until suddenly, just when you think there's no way Herzog could possibly get some Malickian wide-angle lens nature/animal/cave stuff into this story, it is an extremely lyrical and moving movie about those sisters' tunnel-digging search for a better (dream) world where they can be free. The Mara's are excellent (every bit of overlapping dialogue and body language expertly controlled) but it was the dedication to David Lynch that kinda unlocked this for me. Whose weirdness allowed them to uncover the enormity and beauty of the world more than his?
