2026-10-08 Matchbox the Movie (2026) davidehrlich secondary 1.5 no Credit where credit’s due: For a film that should never have been made or released in the first place, “Matchbox: The Movie” is somehow arriving at just the right time. Impeccably positioned as a potential harbinger of what’s to come, this bland and frictionless Skydance production — debuting on Apple TV a few short days after “Flyboys” star David Ellison finalized an $111 billion deal to consolidate what’s left of Hollywood under his control — is a perfect crystallization of our new god-king’s taste in cinema. Like “Ghosted,” “The Family Plan,” and “The Family Plan 2” before it (the latter two of which were also written by David Coggeshall), “Matchbox: The Movie” is an algorithmic chore about workaday Americans who get mixed up in some vaguely comedic spy action, the film’s execution so uninspired that its wish fulfillment premise assumes a tone of patronizing condescension — “what if your shitty little life enjoyed a sudden jolt of excitement?” can be a depressing conceit for a movie that can barely imagine that for itself. Like “Heart of Stone,” “Fountain of Youth,” and Michael Bay’s “6 Underground” (remember that one?), it plays less like a self-possessed piece of popcorn entertainment than it does a large adult son’s half-remembered idea of how movies used to feel. Like “Terminator: Dark Fate” and “Without Remorse,” it takes the residual appeal of its IP for granted to the point that it fails to supply any appeal of its own. And though “Matchbox: The Movie” is much better than some of these early Skydance classics, its relative quality clarifies the most worrisome thing that’s shared between them: It’s not that all of them are bad, but rather that none of them seem particularly concerned with being good. ~this review continues on IndieWire~
