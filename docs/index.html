2026-11-12 Spider-Man: Brand New Day (2026) thejoshl primary 3.0 no Shame that it took 4 movies to get here for some reason, but after a decade of Holland's Spider-Man never really getting a proper Peter Parker story (in favor using him as a device for various universe table-setting, meme-generation writing, and generic plug-and-play plotting of any other superhero), I was pleasantly surprised to see my favorite comic book character get some simple, grounded, small-scale work-life balance drama again. Not only is it a better fit for Holland who has always been serviceably charming/funny in the role but is a more capable tortured young actor than I thought, and the same goes for Destin Daniel Cretton whose embarrassing first MCU outing did not remotely play to his strengths: namely textured, melancholy close-ups of young actors being sad (see: Short Term 12 ). The first hour or so of this in particular where Peter is simply suffering from the crippling loneliness of his decision to live a double life, and the sensory confusion of what's happening to his body, I was like... What the hell? Did they know they could've treated these characters like humans with feelings the whole time? Did they just suddenly remember the Raimi ones did this/were good? Or maybe we can chalk it up to the Justin Kuritzkes rewrite? Honestly, I never thought I'd say this about an MCU movie either, but this even has some nice-looking images, in part because of the grounded nature of it all. Simple shots of Holland actually existing in a real city (what a bar this had to clear lol), staring at Zendaya across a house party, riding the subway, going to class, reflecting in a puddle, standing in front of a rooftop neon sign, being depressed in his apartment. I do think unfortunately the MCU proclivity towards being overlong and overstuffing with contractually obligated plot/characters does sink some of it down the stretch. As much as I enjoy Bernthal/Ruffalo/Pugh they just don't need to be here to the extent they are, and the telepathic body-swapping/possession stuff is an interesting obstacle early on and but ultimately ends up feeling a little too much like a Jean Grey Will Return teaser by its conclusion rather than the thematic/therapeutic throughline it's trying to be. The CG pre-vis setpiece pipeline all these movies use is still kinda doing its ugly thing too, though I did enjoy how much of the fight choreography was ripped from the recent video games, and how many of them appeared to take place inside of actual sets being destroyed instead of extended AI desktop landscapes. Anyway, easily the best Holland Spider-Man, and nice to actually like one of these again.
