2026-09-04 The Love Witch (2016) thejoshl primary 4.0 no Plenty of filmmakers have playfully weaponized retro modes of filmmaking as a means of subversively satirizing them, but what Biller does here in terms of putting feminist gender war theory up against some of the most lovingly hand-crafted 60s pastiche I've ever seen (everything from pulp fiction, sexploitation, melodramas, Hammer/Italian horror, Technicolor Hitchcock) is nothing short of astonishing to look at. The Victorian house set, colorful costumes, occult Tarot card décor, hard lighting, vintage deep focus lensing, repurposed score (including Morricone) etc. is meticulous to the point that it fully earns also having the shaggy pace and stilted acting style these movies often did. Samantha Robinson's strangely, excellently calibrated performance is really the only thing that gives the game away, in a way that feels deliberately anachronistic. She looks and moves like she's Edwige Fenech or Soledad Miranda but her attitude and speaking manner expose it as a garishly manicured surface, a modern woman uncannily playing into old-fashioned sexist male fantasy iconography as both a curse put upon her but also a glamourous burlesque tool she can assume ownership of in the form of a sociopathic man-eating succubus. The same qualities that make her this sex (magic) object men can project onto are also what they eventually find scary about her and are sent into fits of suicidal madness and depression over, and her self-awareness of this becomes her power, her weapon. Biller is achieving a similar power with her intensely stylized formal construction, like a potion she's brewing or spell she's casting with the artificial dreaminess of this gorgeous film language intrinsically tied to dated, regressive views on gender.
