2026-09-18 Arrested Memory (2026) thejoshl primary 4.0 no Japanese actor-turned-director SABU's latest has an easy high-concept logline to pitch you: a Judo-throwing detective traumatized by the Mission Impossible massacre of his team is inflicted by "stress-induced amnesia" while in the middle of trying to solve a high-profile High and Low kidnapping case. It's a premise the lead Taiwanese actor Ethan Juan takes completely dramatically seriously, constantly experiencing guilt-ridden flashbacks and disorienting subjective memory lapses as he existentially questions his identity and job. Which makes it even funnier when SABU throws him into and surrounds him with some of the keenest sense of irrational cop movie/Miike yakuza/run-all-night thriller slapstick I've seen in some time. Once it gets its set-up out of the way, this functionally turns into 70 straight minutes of that scene in Memento where Guy Pearce wakes up in the middle of a foot chase and goes "Okay, so what am I doing? I'm chasing this guy. Nope... He's chasing me." With the detective going through a series of cliché scenes he's expected to: driving to the scene of the crime, doing money exchanges with criminals, rusty warehouse action finale, etc but constantly forgetting where he is, why he's there, who he's meeting, and most importantly who he is. It's a complete riot to watch as SABU expertly maintains the constant sense of escalation and accumulation, often taking one miscommunication gag or comedic member of the ensemble or deadpan reaction and strategically overlapping it with the next so it feels like one long extended action-comedy setpiece, and beautifully shooting it as a hazy, fuzzy, and romantically refracted slow-motion neon-noir. Often having his detective get through the legitimately farcical situations he finds himself in through sheer muscle memory instinct to help others, Judo flip bad guys Throw Down style, and a bit of luck + rationalization by his underlings who either view his strange behavior as the work of a rogue traitor or a genius undercover operator. There are too many hilarious stretches in such an economical runtime to catalog them in a review but some highlights: having his case taken over by a meth head he picked up while forgetting where he was driving, missing his exit/crashing his car and becoming romantically involved with a suicidal maid who he gives some of the ransom cash to not remembering why he's carrying a bag full of money (the halo light on her face revealed to be bus headlights!!), kicking down a hotel door thinking there's a serial killer in there and questioning whether the guy who goes through the window was just a random civilian, befriending and being helped by one of the kidnappers not realizing who each other are, and getting into a literal "Who's on first?" cellphone calling routine with him. Really wasn't expecting this to be more of a comedy than a martial arts film, but it's a truly excellent comedy (lost it at the bag picking up fetish and bilingual housekeeper meth-buying bit), while never losing sight of the "please remember me" identity crisis melodrama all the gags are branching out from. The balancing act of absurdism and emotion best exemplified by a surreal moment where he hallucinates the ghosts of all of his dead team members opening the doors to his van while he's driving in a neon green tunnel thinking about them, and the scene just keeps going like a reverse clown car. Also features an incredible nightclub dance sequence and somehow a completely earned rom-com wrap-up ending.
2026-09-18 Resident Evil (2026) silentdawn secondary 2.5 no 45 Going to be a little bit of a grouch about this one but honestly, fuck it. Didn't think I'd ponder selling my Cregger stock after the wicked delights of Barbarian , but the diminishing returns of both Weapons and this has me considering otherwise. It's a decent romp to be sure, and the logline of some Random Guy having to deal with the T-virus is a genuinely good bit, but it's stretched way too thin, even at 90 minutes. Not exactly a bad movie, but pretty frivolous and repetitive, especially in the back half. Cregger's confident direction is still the highlight, with the initial accident-cop-farmhouse sequence being a great example of how to properly translate gaming language into cinematic narrative stakes. What this movie excels at is visualizing the feeling of being scared while playing a horror title and conveying that through its Anybody protagonist. It works for awhile, but the incessant and uneven comedy poisons the well by the third act. For every earned humorous moment, there's two or three instances of 'holy fucktffuck shittt fuck' blabbering that slowly started to grate on my nerves. Even worse was that "video games!!" moment - unbearable and an apt example of the quirked-up millennial self-awareness throughout that ultimately only services the Intellectual Property of it all. Surprised that RE superfans are so angry at this movie because it's basically two degrees away from pure franchise slop. Well directed, sure, but it struggles to ever be more than a series of stitched-together set-pieces of a Twitch streamer stuck in a biohazard nightmare. Maybe I just have the cilantro gene with Cregger's sensibility, I don't know. Disappointed.
