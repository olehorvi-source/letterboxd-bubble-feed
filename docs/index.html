2026-10-18 You Can See Everything (2026) thejoshl primary 5.0 no What happens when an unstoppable force meets an immovable object? In their own individual way, both Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim have been exploring the concept and method of not just docu-fiction but "docu-fantasia". A form of documentary filmmaking that accepts that the camera inherently lies, corrupts, or at the very least affects the truth of any reality you hope to document; and instead of viewing that as a limitation, taking it a step further and incorporating that fact as a deliberate tool to further explore and express. With Fielder that takes the shape of elaborate, absurdist simulations of human behavior that began as deadpan cringe-comic satire of reality TV and eventually morphed into legitimate psychological studies of the strange, awkward, lonely, and performative ways people go about trying to make connections with each other (including himself). For Lance, it's been more about an overt tinkering with fictional cinematic style applied to real subjects as a means of subjectively accessing the way they see the world. It probably goes without saying that they are the two absolute weirdest filmmakers someone about to go to prison could possibly reach out to surveil them for a month to (presumably... ostensibly...) if not prove their innocence, at least take back control of their image, but that's what infamous Silicon Valley fraudster Elizabeth Holmes did. The results are one of the most simultaneously intimate and confounding works of filmmaking I've ever seen. One that for 90 minutes plays almost exactly as I went in expecting. With Fielder comically inserted into Holmes' domestic space, both intensely skeptical of everything he's seeing and being told by people he believes to be outright liars, but so impressed at the "radical transparency" she and her partner Billy Evans (sneakily just as psychologically fascinating a subject as she is) are offering him that he can't stop himself from watching, listening, maybe even occasionally doubting what he thinks he knows about them (as well as truth, reality...) That confused, voyeuristic thrill is extended to us as we watch Nathan sometimes sit back observe how this couple operates as lovers/parents/business partners, allowing them to fill the silence with the performance of themselves they want the cameras to see until Nathan spots a stray, strange thread they accidentally (?) leave there and tugs at it. My favorite example early on is how hung up and aggressive he gets about Billy's mid-conversation instinct to take the egg whisking bowl out of Holmes hand to do it himself, which he clocks as a microcosm of what they're "real" relationship might be. It quickly becomes clear that Nathan, a master manipulator unlike anything this medium has seen since Hitchcock or Welles, has become obsessed with someone he thinks is also a master manipulator unlike anything he's ever met. Holmes’ doe eyes, perpetual smile, on-command tears, the way every word out of her mouth (even ones we know to be false) feel both vacantly alien but also completely authentic. If she isn’t telling the truth than maybe she is telling her own self-deluded truth? It can’t really be that simple, can it? I haven’t been able to stop replaying in my mind Nathan’s baffled, character-breaking “c’mon, Liz…” at her claim that she can’t recall ever telling a lie before in her adult life; a challenge directly to him he hopes to catch at some point but never manages to get an on-camera smoking gun. Or the later one of him likening talking to her with the famous Abbot and Costello routine “Who’s On First?” Which he does as a metaphor for her argument that she didn’t knowingly deceive investors about her product, merely accidentally miscommunicated and confused them with her choice of words (after all, the movie itself ambiguously opens and playfully closes with an apparent misquote), only for it to devolve into a comical misunderstanding about comedy itself, and whether she even understands the joke let alone the metaphor… “She just thinks differently.” It’s an enthralling documentary during this entire stretch, but what escalates it to unfathomable greatness is the second half. Where Nathan loses his access to his subject due to the prison sentence anxiously looming over the first and begins to spiral trying to make sense of what he’s seen, heard and photographed thus far. Realizing not only has he come away with no clear revelations about this situation, but he’s been left with far more frustratingly, tantalizing questions than he had before he even started. Is this a simple textbook case of delusional narcissism that has allowed her to believe her own lies and thus restate them to his face (and camera) with convincing conviction? Or is she an Oscar-worthy performer putting on a 24/7 act as committed as anything we see out of the magicians from The Prestige ? What is the true nature of her relationship with Billy? Is he a dominant, predatory tech bro in a long line of abusive partners who have taken advantage of her? Or is he the exact type of business-savvy control freak she deliberately aligns herself with in order to keep her hands/conscious clean? Is it a little of both? Is it possible for it to be both, and if so, is it such a perfect freak-matching compromise/partnership that it’s actually even a little legitimately romantic in a fucked-up way (a la Phantom Thread or Gone Girl —complete with shower dong)? Nathan spends the entire second half reviewing his own footage trying to find the answers to these (and more), trying to see whether there’s a hidden take or different angle/interpretation that could give him some clarity. Going as far as to call in [redacted Oscar-worthy collaborator] to help process and recreate footage he couldn’t get from (imperfect, biased) memory, which shouldn't be a surprise for anyone who's seen what he's been up to recently on The Rehearsal . Going not just full Kiarostami fact-and-fiction blurring mode (that both questions the ethics of his own uncomfortable close-up methods but also admits that the best filmmakers are gifted liars who use it as a tool to locate some sort of cathartic truth even if it’s not the objective truth), but also borderline Vertigo in his obsessive quest to rebuild, restage and fantasize about the absent blonde cipher he’s had taken away from him. The extent to which this becomes just as much about his own psychological obsession and need for some sort of closure can’t be understated, as it does truly unravel into meta-surveillance, verité hall-of-mirrors magic trick of a movie. With Fielder invited into the close and closed off orbit of Holmes’ reality she and Billy may or may not be choosing to believe/trying (and failing) to impose on the world through whatever channels of wealth/power they have, and if not falling for it at the very least tripping over it or humoring it as a possibility, as Oppenheim tries to film his collaborators loss of footing/objectivity from a distance. I will be thinking about (and reviewing in my own mind) for some time the playfully disorienting crosscutting during that final artificially realized, supposedly real conversation between Fielder and Holmes, and the easy out/explanation he tries to provide her with to use, only for her to bizarrely sidestep him entirely and deliver the most perfectly rehearsed or genuine cosmic expression (or… monument?) of love he’s ever born witness to. Also all the AI/Twitter Holmes stuff... Whether this is a document (monument) to the impossibility/unknowability of ever fully knowing what’s going on inside someone else’s mind, or an intimate portrait of a narcissistic grifter by a master manipulator whose finally met his match, the one thing that’s for sure is that, well, it’s certainly “very complicated.”
