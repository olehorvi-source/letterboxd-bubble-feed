2026-09-19 Mr. Nelson, Did You Kill People? (2026) thejoshl primary 3.0 no Shinya Tsukamoto's Late Style anti-war era continues with this heavy-handed and unpleasant adaptation of the autobiography of black Vietnam veteran turned anti-war activist and lecturer Allen Nelson who apparently gave over 1,200 talks in Japan and is now buried there, and is played in the film with uncomfortable and admirable intensity by Rodney Hicks. For those unfamiliar with how Tsukamoto's sensibility has evolved since Tetsuo: The Iron Man , it may came as a surprise that that the vulgar and violent cyberpunk body horror auteur is making a film that has a few scenes you could ungenerously call a Lifetime remake of Casualties of War , but if you've been following him at all you'll know that he's been playing around for some time now with applying his uniquely upsetting and disturbing forms of violence (both bodily and psychological) to more conventional forms of genre storytelling ( Killing , for example, was his first time taking on a samurai story), but with the explicit agenda of addressing the historical perpetrators of violent atrocities and the rippling, haunting effects they've had on the world. Mr. Nelson certainly has some obvious flaws: the cheap digital look, awkward pacing, questionable supporting performances (Geoffrey Rush a few times gets really close to going "Yarr, Matey Nelson, Did Ya Kill People?!"), but I was surprised by how little they affected the overall depth of feeling Tsukamoto and Hicks clearly have for this material and this man. Even if how it goes about conveying it is earnest to a fault, the idea that talking and confession is a form of healing is quite powerful by the end, while also acknowledging that isn't enough and that the brutal memories of victims will and deserve to endure. Few filmmakers know how to deploy the shaky, scratchy abstract close-up art of gushing wounds and piles of corpses quite like Tsukamoto does, because sometimes to take misery to that extra level you need to throw in some repulsion.
