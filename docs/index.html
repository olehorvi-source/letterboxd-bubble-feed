Hardly anything in this movie is left hidden for the audience to uncover. All it offers are countless congratulatory layers of Slasher self-awareness for an exercise that is ostensibly about as "clever" as one of those Fear Street installments on Netflix. Actually kind of shocked at how bad this is, as both of Jane Shoenburn's prior works impressed me quite a bit. Stale horror genre commentary delivered in post-modern millennial speak in an attempt to weaponize our relationship towards flesh and franchises. Snooooore. Even worse are the horror sequences, which take on an uncanny blend of fetishistic 80s minutiae and sloppy digitality to balance the memories and realities of our nostalgic favorites. Can we please, please close the book on examining our own self-realizations as by-products of the media we consume? More than a few of the thematic conclusions reached in this film gave me....pause, especially in terms of our collective tendency to attach ourselves emotionally to the fantasies we surround ourselves with. A slippery premise that is ultimately handled with the grace and poise of a Shudder Original. That Zoom call scene had me cringing the fuck out. And all of the candy shit? Sugary unfunny filler for an academic essay that still feels like a first draft, with no cogent thesis beyond the usual laundry list of metatextual concerns. Not on board with any of this, sorry. Just stay home and watch Sleepaway Camp .

Say what you will about Blackberry also being a biopic that takes artistic liberty to bend its subject to the autobiographical "man-child who fucks up and learns a lesson through failure" story MJ is attracted to exploring, I wouldn't have mistaken scenes in that movie for 100 other coming-of-age dramedies you've seen come out of Sundance for 30+ years. He hits a number of these beats perfectly pleasantly (and sadly), Bauman gets some really nice photography of Cape Cod, and Matt gets at least two legit terrific periphery performances out of the environment he creates for his actors; Banderas who elevates his religiously strict but genuinely loving mentor role with real weariness, and Woodall who leans into the sad dirtbag quality of his coked-out, womanizing blue-collar chowderhead (not regretting the stock I bought on this guy when I saw Tuner last year). But ultimately, as much as I enjoyed hanging out and laughing with these guys (shoutout Stavvy Baby), and liked how it looked and sounded (great needledrops, good score by Jay), there's a pervasive formulaic plainness to this that felt like an outsider filmmaker trying to prove that they can behave and deliver a conventional product if you were to let them audition for it.
