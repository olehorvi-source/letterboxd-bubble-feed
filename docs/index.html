2026-10-25 Exorcist II: The Heretic (1977) brofromanother primary no The standard cinephile line is that Ex 2 functions as a corrective to its predecessor - that Boorman’s earnest, flailing folly stands in opposition to Friedkin’s savvy, machine-tooled brutality. There’s something to this insofar as Boorman more or less went on the record about his against-the-grain intentions, while Friedkin registered his displeasure with the results in the same mocking terms as the majority of film critics descending, Pazazu-like, on a DOA sequel: “the worst piece of shit I’ve ever seen..a fucking disgrace… your mother sucks cocks in hell, Boorman.” Me, I’m built different. I like both movies, and also Blatty’s weird and wacky Exorcist III - itself a departure, heralding a decade and change of demonic urban- inferno procedurals (Seven, Cure, etc). My fondness for The Heretic is bound up mostly in its aesthetics, which are - as per authorities from Pauline Kael to Martin Scorsese - flatly stunning, though it’s worth noting that they often honour and extrapolate on the source material: the tug of war over Regan’s heart echoes the shot of her in the original reaching out while the statue of Pazazu lurks behind her. The sleepwalking sequence atop the skyscraper is so beautiful: modernism refracted and made uncanny , with a long way to fall. The shooting, the cutting, the music, the vibes, the colours, children - all gorgeous. I can recognize the lurching tone, and the disinterested bluster of Burton’s acting but I think it all suits the hokum just fine; the induced- somnambulism subplot renders cinema and spectatorship as rich and medium-specific subtext (De Palma did the exact same thing, the same year, in The Fury - another movie goofs laugh at instead of with). The recent doc is fine but suspended a bit awkwardly between advocacy and mockery; Boorman, and the devil both deserve better but rest assured Owen Glieberman’s smarmy, Monkey’s-Paulette review will not see heaven.
