2026-11-04 Rope (1948) thejoshl primary 4.0 yes “Murder can be an art too… The power to kill can be just as satisfying as the power to create.” It makes a lot of sense that an adaptation of a stage play inspired by a real-life gay couple who committed murder as an intellectual exercise would itself be treated as a formal exercise by the master of the visual language of murder stories. The elegant, gliding long-take oner illusion (assembled from a dozen or so shots) is occasionally stretched beyond the point of claustrophobic pressure cooker effectiveness, but it’s a lot less ostentatiously gimmicky than its reputation. Hitchcock for the most part keeps his fluid camera movement economical and functional, drifting in and out of anxious character perspective in a way that mostly sustains the morbid, real-time tension of Brandon and Phillip’s comically perverse dinner party where a bunch of bitchy melodrama is infected by the dramatic irony of us knowing where the strangled corpse in the room is right from the opening shot. It’s a cool subversion of the murder mystery genre to make it not really a mystery at all and instead focus on the comedy of manners between this subtextually (but also hilariously obviously) gay couple and the guests they're hosting, as well as the (im)moral Nietzschean philosophizing/sociopathic horniness that inspired their inciting crime. How often that wooden chest hangs out on the periphery of the frame as guilt continues to mount, or the camera suddenly adopts the POV focus of incriminating objects as they become fearful of their discovery is where this technique is working at its finest. I particularly like the bit in where Rupert theorizes the process of how to commit a murder in such a space out loud to the killers, and the camera adopts his sightlines as he goes step-by-step through it; arriving at the hiding of the body part and clearly looking towards chest while deceptively denying the conclusion we just saw him come to and saying he would actually take the corpse out the door. Despite his own retrospective objections to the casting, Jimmy Stewart is very good at doing this suspicious, perceptive detective routine, observing the bizarre details and seams of this elaborately “perfect” murder plan and piecing it together, but I do find his didactic moral outrage arc over these wealthy delusional murderers quite silly... He goes from being like “murder is so sick, it’s awesome as hell, a work of art in many ways, we should start doing The Purge” to [sees dead body for the first time] “wait wtf this sucks ass I’m sorry I’m trying to delete it” so quickly I've never really fully bought it. Anyway, sick movie about doing crimes with your annoying buddy. And that movable multi-room set is unbelievable; the nighttime skyline painting with glowing neon signs leaking through the windows during the finale is beautiful.
