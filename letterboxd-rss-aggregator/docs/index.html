2026-08-02 Spider-Man 2 (2004) thejoshl primary 5.0 yes This is definitely the most watched DVD of my life right here, which I realized a few minutes in when I still had the timing of lines and shots memorized. ("Whoooaaaaa, he stole that guy's pizzas!") Which probably explains why I've not revisited it all that much—it's like I saw it yesterday! Anyway, alongside Batman Returns this is still the high point of the entire genre and trend (imo). On top of being a genius at assembling all the kinetic, acrobatic practical + CG setpieces, and throwing some of his horror chops into the mix (the Doc Ock surgery lab massacre sequence legit scared me as a kid, long before I knew what Evil Dead even was; the puppetry work, shadows, shrieking sound design my god), what's always been most special about these is how much of an earnest dork Raimi is. There's a sheer glee and passion for this character on display, and not just what makes him a fun action hero but the human melodrama and humor of his extreme work-personal life balance dilemma. My ideal Peter Parker is a working-class bozo who gets into slapstick antics trying to evade his rent-demanding landlord and has a genuinely heartbreakingly tortured friend/family/love life. He gets his head slapped around by backpacks while already late for his class, he tries to get into poetry while doing his laundry to impress a girl he's already lost, he tries and fails to help Aunt May apply for a bank loan so she won't lose her home... I can't even imagine one of these movies nowadays slowing down enough to be an old-fashioned class drama or romance like this, to have close-ups of characters feeling complicated things the script is actually naturally and intimately exploring, not just briefly pausing between action scenes so they can do a "story" as a vehicle for fan-service and meme-generation. To include details like Aunt May breaking down mid-sentence telling Peter to take the $20 bill for his birthday, or that brief POV shot of him looking in his dingy apartment closet at his two suits, or the sweet and goofy "Raindrops Keep Falling On My Head" rom-com montage. What's most astonishing to see now is that none of the jokes are meta/sarcastic quips either, here they're often physical gags based in character and if they are verbal they're based in the colorful, memorable personalities of the supporting cast. Even in the roles small enough to warrant one scene/line: Campbell's annoying usher, "any more bright ideas?" train guy. Too many to list out. I'm sure everyone's got their favorite. Molina is also just inspired casting for the sympathetic mad scientist whose genius and ego lead him down a path of miserable and destructive power-seeking, and serves as a dangerously irresponsible counterpart to Peter's selfish (but completely emotionally understandable) desire to sacrifice his power to live an ordinary college kid life. Tobey's big kitchen confession and his gradual realization after it that he owes it to us to be personally punished for the betterment of society, is arguably the most moving this genre has ever gotten. “Sometimes to do what’s right we have to be steady, and give up the thing we want the most. Even our dreams." [Checks notes] The guy who wrote Paper Moon wrote this?! And I mean, c'mon, at the end of the day it's the train setpiece... right? Just the greatest of its kind on-screen. It's an astonishing technical feat, Elfman's rousing score goes crazy, but even moreso it gets me misty-eyed about superheroism (a concept this reckons with seriously, sincerely, romantically, philosophically) in the way only this character does for me. "He's... just a... kid." When Dunst literally glows in his apartment doorframe before the "go get 'em, tiger"; a genuinely beautiful image.
